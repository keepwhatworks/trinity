About

Keep What Works is one writer's working notebook made public: essays on building durable systems, written from inside the work rather than above it. The essays share a spine — twelve principles, each earned by something breaking — and the site keeps them honest with executable checks: claims trace to instruments, numbers trace to measurements, and a test suite reds when the copy outruns the evidence.

The tool that fell out of writing them is Trinity Local: send one prompt to Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini in parallel, see what they agree on and where they split, and let a chairman synthesize a verdict weighted by which model your own decisions historically side with. It is free, MIT-licensed, local-first, and runs on the subscriptions you already pay for. No lab can build it, because no lab can see across all three — that asymmetry is the whole idea, and the essays explain why.

The name is the method: throw what breaks, keep what works, and make "works" mean something you can measure. The essays are the long version. The story is the short one.

Written and built by Vishi Gondi. The code, the docs, and the receipts are on GitHub.
